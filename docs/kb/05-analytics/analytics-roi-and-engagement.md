# ROI, Cost Analytics, and Engagement

## Understanding ROI in DoorKnocker

DoorKnocker tracks costs precisely, but ROI calculation requires you to tell the system what a lead is worth to your business. Set your **estimated value per lead** in Settings — this number drives the ROI estimate in the analytics dashboard.

**Estimated ROI formula:**
> (Leads Generated × Estimated Lead Value) − Total Spend = Estimated Net Return

This is an estimate. Your actual ROI depends on how many leads actually convert to customers, which DoorKnocker cannot track directly (it only knows about QR scans, not closed deals).

---

## QR Scan Rate

The QR scan rate is the most reliable engagement signal DoorKnocker can measure:

**QR Scan Rate = (Total QR Scans ÷ Total Postcards Sent) × 100%**

Industry benchmarks for direct mail QR scan rates vary by industry, but anything above 1–2% is generally considered solid performance. Home services campaigns often see 2–5% scan rates.

**Factors that affect scan rate:**
- Offer clarity and attractiveness
- Time of year (seasonality)
- Postcard design and QR code visibility
- Geographic area (some neighborhoods respond better than others)

---

## Cost Per Postcard

This is the average cost for each postcard sent across a campaign or set of campaigns. It equals the paper quality price ($3.00 or $3.50) minus any coupon discount applied.

---

## Cost Per Lead

**Cost Per Lead = Total Campaign Spend ÷ Leads Generated**

"Leads generated" in DoorKnocker means: referrals linked to campaigns that transitioned to "In Use" status. This is a proxy for intent, not a guaranteed conversion.

---

## Delivery Rate

**Delivery Rate = Postcards with "Completed" status ÷ Total Postcards Sent**

A good delivery rate is typically 95%+ for verified address lists. Lower rates may indicate:
- A high number of addresses that were verified but still returned (e.g., vacancies that opened after verification)
- A geographic area with high turnover rates

## Engagement Deep Dive

The Engagement Deep Dive tab breaks down when and where recipients interact with your postcards:

**Time patterns**: a chart showing when QR scans happen during the day and week. Many direct mail recipients scan postcards in the evening (6–9pm). Use this to time follow-up calls or social media ads.

**Geographic heatmap**: a map showing where scans cluster. Compare this to your zone boundary — if scans are concentrated in one part of the zone and absent in another, your next campaign could target the high-response area more precisely.

---

## Improving Performance

If scan rates are low:
1. Review the postcard design — is the QR code prominent and does the offer have a clear benefit?
2. Check the offer — a discount, free estimate, or limited-time offer outperforms generic "learn more" CTAs
3. Try a different geographic area — not all neighborhoods respond equally
4. Test different postcard sizes — larger postcards (6×11) have more space to make the offer compelling
